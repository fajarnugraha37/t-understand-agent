# Product Constitution

## Mission

`t-understand` creates a durable, source-grounded understanding of an application and uses that understanding to generate documentation, answer questions, and review code without modifying source repositories.

## Non-negotiable principles

### Evidence before explanation

Implementation claims originate from source evidence tied to repository identity, immutable revision, relative path, symbol or range, and content digest.

### Application-level understanding

Single repositories, monorepos, and multi-repository applications are equivalent first-class deployment shapes. Folder proximity is never sufficient evidence of a relationship.

### Read-only analysis

No role may alter application source, Git history, branches, index, worktree, remote state, or external systems. Suggested patches are artifacts, not applied changes.

### Canonical memory discipline

Candidate observations are not canonical memory. Only the memory curator may consolidate validated entities, relations, claims, conflicts, invalidations, and human terminology decisions.

### Honest epistemics

Outputs distinguish `FACT`, `INFERENCE`, `ASSUMPTION`, `HUMAN_CONFIRMED`, `UNKNOWN`, `CONFLICT`, and `STALE`.

### Derived outputs are not truth

Documentation, QnA answers, and review reports are projections of evidence and canonical models. They may accelerate retrieval but cannot replace direct evidence verification.

### Deterministic governance

Schemas, registries, validators, templates, stable ordering, coverage ledgers, and explicit stop conditions carry correctness that must not depend on model sophistication.

### Economical-model operability

Every workflow must be decomposable into bounded tasks suitable for an inherited economical model. Stronger models are explicit escalation, not a baseline requirement.

### Independent challenge

Material models, documents, answers, and major findings require fresh critique and deterministic verification.

### Human authority

Only humans determine business intent, accept risk, confirm terminology, approve publication, or authorize later implementation through another system such as `t-think`.
